Getting Around Austin: Transport, Parking & Airport Guide

Kypier Guides austin November 20, 2026 4 min read
Austin-Bergstrom International Airport (AUS) sits 8 miles southeast of downtown. The city lacks a direct rail link to the airport, making rideshares, taxis, and rental cars the primary options. CapMetro's Route 20 bus connects AUS to downtown for $1.25. Car ownership or rideshare remains the most practical way to navigate Austin's spread-out layout.

Austin Airport (AUS): Getting to Your Rental

Austin-Bergstrom International Airport is served by all major rental car companies operating from the on-site Rental Car Facility, a short walk from the Barbara Jordan Terminal. Rideshare pickups are on the lower level, curbside at the designated TNCs zone. Expect $25–$45 to downtown by Uber or Lyft; fares spike during SXSW, ACL, and F1 weekend. The Airport Flyer (Route 20) departs every 30 minutes from the ground transportation curb, costs $1.25, and stops at key downtown transit hubs including the CapMetro Plaza on Congress Avenue. Shared shuttle services like SuperShuttle operate but require advance booking.

Public Transportation: Bus, MetroRapid & Rail Options

CapMetro runs Austin's bus network with a flat $1.25 fare per ride using the CapMetro app or an EZ Rider card. The two most useful MetroRapid lines for visitors are Route 801, which runs the full length of South Congress Avenue and North Lamar Boulevard, and Route 803 along Burnet Road and South 1st Street. The MetroRail Red Line connects downtown's Lakeline Station to Leander — useful for North Austin, not central neighborhoods. All-day passes cost $2.50. Frequency drops sharply after 9pm and on weekends, limiting reliability for evening use.

Ride-Sharing, Taxis & Car Rentals in Austin

Uber and Lyft are the dominant ground transport options throughout Austin. Base fares within central Austin typically run $10–$18; airport runs average $30–$40. Taxis are available via Yellow Cab Austin but rarely hailed street-side — book by phone or app. For stays longer than three days or for exploring beyond the urban core, renting a car is worthwhile. Enterprise, Hertz, and Avis all have locations at AUS and several off-airport sites along Airport Boulevard and near The Domain. Reserve in advance during major events when both rideshare surge pricing and rental inventory spike dramatically.

Parking in Austin: Street Parking, Lots & Neighbourhood Tips

Downtown street parking is metered through the ParkATX system at $1.50–$3.00 per hour, enforced 8am–10pm Monday through Saturday. The Republic Square Garage on West 4th Street and the Brazos Street Garage offer structured parking at $2–$5/hr with evening flat rates around $10–$15. In East Austin along East 6th Street and East Cesar Chavez, street parking is free but competitive on weekends. South Congress Avenue has limited free side-street parking on Travis Heights blocks. Permit-only zones are marked clearly; violations start at $65. Using the SpotHero app to pre-book downtown garages saves both time and cost.

Getting Around by Bike: Trails & Bike Share

MetroBike (formerly BCycle) operates 70+ stations across central Austin, with single rides from $3.50 for 30 minutes and day passes at $15. The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail — a 10-mile loop around Lady Bird Lake — is the central cycling artery, connecting Zilker Park to the East Side and downtown. Dedicated lanes run along Guadalupe Street, Barton Springs Road, and portions of South Congress. Bike commuting between South Lamar and the UT campus is practical and faster than driving during peak hours. Helmet use is not legally required for adults but strongly advisable.

Day Trip Transportation: Driving Routes & Tips

Austin's central location makes several day trips straightforward by car. San Antonio is 80 miles south via I-35, roughly 1.5 hours without traffic. The Texas Hill Country — including Fredericksburg and Wimberley — lies 60–90 minutes west on US-290 and TX-12. Houston is 165 miles east on I-10, approximately 2.5 hours. For Enchanted Rock State Natural Area, allow 90 minutes via US-290 W. Avoid I-35 through downtown Austin between 7–9am and 4–7pm on weekdays; MoPac Expressway (Loop 1) is a faster north-south alternative. Tolls on TX-130 range from $3–$8 and reliably bypass the I-35 corridor.

Knowing how you'll move through Austin before you arrive turns what can be a frustrating city to navigate into one that's genuinely manageable on your own terms.
