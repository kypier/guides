Where to Stay in Austin: Neighbourhood Guide

Kypier Guides austin November 21, 2026 4 min read
Downtown and South Congress are the top bases for most Austin visitors. Downtown puts you within walking distance of Sixth Street bars, the Convention Center, and Lady Bird Lake in under 10 minutes. South Congress suits travellers who want independent restaurants, vintage shops, and a slower pace. East Austin is the pick for nightlife without the tourist crowds.

Downtown Austin: walkable nightlife and attractions

The core of Downtown runs between Congress Avenue and Red River Street, anchored by Sixth Street to the north and Lady Bird Lake to the south. You can walk from the Texas State Capitol to the Moody Center in about 25 minutes. Hotels here include the Fairmont Austin, Line Austin, and JW Marriott, all within two blocks of Rainey Street. Rainey Street is the more liveable bar strip, with bungalow bars like Banger's and Brew Exchange. Convention Center events and live music at ACL Live at the Moody Theater are a short walk from most Downtown hotels. Rates spike hard during SXSW, often doubling from base prices.

South Congress: trendy shops, food, and local culture

South Congress Avenue (SoCo) runs south from the river and concentrates most of its activity between Oltorf Street and Elizabeth Street, roughly a 1.5-mile strip. Hotel San José (from $200/night) and the South Congress Hotel sit directly on the strip. Restaurants worth noting: Perla's for Gulf seafood, Homeslice Pizza for late-night slices, and Güero's Taco Bar, which has been on the strip since 1986. Allen's Boots and Uncommon Objects anchor the retail end. The walk across the Congress Avenue Bridge to Downtown takes about 15 minutes. Parking is tight on weekend evenings.

East Austin: emerging creative scene and neighbourhood vibes

East Austin centres on East 6th Street and East Cesar Chavez Street, both of which have shifted significantly since 2010. This is where most of Austin's newer independent bars and restaurants have opened: Whisler's, Nickel City, Contigo Austin, and Launderette are all within a half-mile of each other. Short-term rentals dominate accommodation here rather than hotels, with most Airbnb and Vrbo options running $120–$180/night for a full house. It is 1.5 miles from East 6th to Downtown, a 10-minute Lyft or 30-minute walk. Noise from bars ends around 2 a.m. on weekends, which is worth factoring in if you book close to 6th Street.

North Austin: affordable option near UT and tech corridor

North Austin covers Hyde Park, the University of Texas campus area, and the stretch of North Lamar up to The Domain. Hyde Park, roughly bounded by 38th and 51st Streets, has the most residential feel with bungalows and local cafes like Quack's Bakery on 43rd. The Domain, 10 miles north of Downtown, is a mixed-use development with Marriott and Aloft properties from $95–$140/night, convenient if your trip involves the Apple, Dell, or Indeed campuses nearby. MetroRapid Bus 803 runs from The Domain to Downtown in around 35 minutes. This area suits budget travellers or those attending UT events.

West Lake Hills: luxury and outdoor access

West Lake Hills sits west of Downtown across the Barton Creek Greenbelt, roughly 7 to 10 miles from Congress Avenue. It is primarily residential, so accommodation means high-end vacation rentals, some listed at $400 to $600 per night for a house with a pool. The draw is direct access to Barton Creek Greenbelt trailheads and proximity to Lake Austin. It is not walkable to anything, and you will need a car for every trip. The Four Seasons Austin, while technically Downtown on the lake, draws a similar luxury traveller who wants proximity to the water. West Lake Hills is best suited to groups or families who want space and quiet.
